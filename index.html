Attention Mechanisms

Queries, Keys,
and Values

Understand the database-like retrieval mechanism that lets a model
search its own context
for the features it actually needs.

Syllabus

Topics

Sections 01 to 11 build the mechanism from scratch. Sections 12 to 15 take it to time series, where the sequence is a signal rather than a sentence.

01

Why Attention?

The fixed-vector bottleneck, and the idea of letting a model look things up instead of memorising.

02

The Dictionary Analogy

Queries, keys, and values: a soft dictionary lookup where every entry is allowed to answer a little.

03

Measuring Similarity

Dot products, projections, and what it actually means for a key to match a query.

04

From Scores to Weights

Softmax, sharpness, and why we divide the scores by the square root of the key dimension.

05

Scaled Dot-Product Attention

Assembling the full formula and reading it as a pipeline of three matrix multiplications.

06

Self-Attention

When the queries, the keys, and the values are all learned projections of the same sequence.

07

Multi-Head Attention

Many lookups in parallel, so that different heads can ask different questions of the same context.

08

Masking and Causality

Padding masks, causal masks, and how we forbid a query from reading the future.

09

Positional Information

Attention cannot see order on its own: sinusoidal encodings, learned embeddings, and rotary positions.

10

The Transformer Block

The residual stream, layer normalisation, and the feed-forward layer that surrounds attention.

11

The Cost of Looking Everywhere

Quadratic complexity, the KV cache, and what linear and flash attention really change.

12

Attention for Time Series

Why forecasting is a retrieval problem, and what breaks when tokens become timestamps.

13

Patching and Channel Independence

Turning a long signal into a short sequence of patches that attention can afford to read.

14

Building a Forecaster

An end-to-end patch attention model on a real series, from the data loader to the attention map.

15

Reading Attention Maps

What the weights do and do not tell you, and when a linear baseline still wins.
